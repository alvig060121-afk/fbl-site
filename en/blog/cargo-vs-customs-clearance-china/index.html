Cargo or Full Customs Clearance: Two Ways to Import from China into Russia

When you import goods from China into Russia, you can choose between two formats: cargo and full customs clearance (known in Russia as "white" delivery). At FBL Group, the two differ in how the goods are cleared; the transport and route can be the same. With cargo, the carrier clears the goods and you do not have to deal with the paperwork. Full clearance involves a customs declaration, payment of customs charges and import documents.

24 November 2026·5 min read

Our transit times are the same for both formats, so the choice comes down to whether you need import documents for the goods. Below we cover how the formats differ, when you need the documents and how to choose. If you would like to discuss your shipment right away, send us a request.

What cargo is

Cargo is the simple option: the carrier clears the goods, and you receive them without handling any customs paperwork yourself. It works well when you do not need import documents in your company's name.

What full customs clearance is

Full clearance means the goods go through complete customs formalities, and a declarant files the customs declaration. Russia is part of the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU). Under its Customs Code, the declarant can be a company or individual in a member state that is a party to the deal with the foreign seller1. That may be your company, or an importer you then buy the goods from.

Customs charges are payable on import. They include import customs duty, import VAT, excise duties and customs fees2. Which of these apply, and at what rates, depends on the product itself. It is best to check this with a customs broker before shipping.

The main difference is the documents you end up with after import. With cargo, the customs declaration is not in your name. With full clearance, the declarant keeps the declaration and proof that the customs charges were paid. If the declarant is an importer, they handle the formalities, and you get documents from them for your purchase of the goods.

Our transit times do not depend on the format. Air takes from 1 day, road from 14 days, rail or sea from 30 days, and full clearance takes just as long. We explain why the actual time can be longer in our article on why China shipments get delayed.

When you need import documents

Selling on Russian marketplaces. Each product category has its own document requirements3. Whether your product needs a certificate or declaration of conformity is checked against the list of goods subject to mandatory certification and declaration3. If it is not on the list, a refusal letter (a document stating that certification is not required) is issued instead3.

The documents for certification include a supply contract or invoice, plus import papers if the goods come from abroad3. If you do not upload these permits, the listing will not be published, or it will be taken down3. On top of the permits, Megamarket often asks for documents confirming supply or import and a copy of the customs declaration for foreign goods3.

VAT deduction. If your company is entitled to deduct VAT, it can deduct the VAT paid on import. When you are the declarant, the deduction is based on documents confirming that the tax was actually paid on import4. When you buy the goods from an importer, the basis is the seller's VAT invoice4. In both cases the goods must first be recorded in your accounts4.

Not every business has this right. For example, businesses on Russia's simplified tax system that apply reduced VAT rates cannot deduct import VAT: it is included in the cost of the goods5. Your accountant can tell you whether the deduction applies to you.

Selling to businesses. If you sell wholesale or to retail chains, the buyer may ask for paperwork on the goods. It is best to ask them in advance which documents they need.

How to choose: four questions

  1. Where will you sell the goods? For a marketplace, check whether your product needs a certificate or declaration and which import papers that will require.
  2. Is your company entitled to deduct VAT? If so, full clearance gives you the documents to deduct it.
  3. Do your buyers need documents? Wholesale buyers and retail chains may ask for them.
  4. How much does simplicity matter? If you do not need import documents, cargo saves you the paperwork.

If you are unsure, tell your logistics manager about the product, the volume and your sales channel, and they can match a format to that. For choosing the transport itself (air, road, rail or sea), see our article on the best shipping method from China. It is also worth inspecting the goods before they ship: see our article on pre-shipment inspection in China.
